# Task: Build the group's first BI layer

**From:** Financial Manager, Washr Group (fictional)
**To:** Adnaan (BI Analyst and Developer)
**Time allowed:** 3 working days

> All data in this folder is dummy data, generated by `generate_data.py`. Washr is modelled here as a 12-branch group so that store-level reporting has something to work with. No real customer, staff or trading data is included.

## Background

The group has 12 branches across Durban and Pietermaritzburg, a mix of company-owned and franchised. Every month-end, finance builds the management pack by hand in Excel from POS exports and the general ledger. It takes four days, the numbers are questioned in every ops meeting, and nobody can see store performance until three weeks after the month closes.

You are the first BI hire. There is no warehouse, no data model and no dashboard. You are building it from scratch.

## What we need

A Power BI report that the Financial Manager and the Operations Manager can open on a Monday and trust.

## Business questions

1. What are Sales, Gross Profit and EBITDA for the group and for each branch, by month and financial year to date, against budget and against last year?
2. Reported sales for March to August 2026 are well up on the same months of 2025. How much of that is real like-for-like growth and how much is new branches?
3. Which branches are underperforming, and is the cause sales, margin or costs? Name the branch and the driver.
4. GP % dipped in 2025. When, why, and has it recovered?
5. Northdale (PMB04) was closed on 30 September 2025. Does the data support that decision?
6. Which store KPIs should ops watch weekly? Propose them, define them and build them.

## Source extracts (in `data/`)

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `pos_sales_FY25.csv`, `pos_sales_FY26.csv`, `pos_sales_FY27_H1.csv` | POS transaction lines, one row per item sold |
| `item_master.csv` | Catalogue export from the accounting system |
| `item_cost_history.csv` | Unit cost per item with effective dates |
| `branches.csv` | Branch list with region, ownership, open and close dates |
| `gl_opex_monthly.csv` | Operating cost lines per branch per month from the general ledger |
| `gl_accounts.csv` | Chart of accounts for those lines |
| `budget_wide.csv` | The budget, exported from the FD's spreadsheet |
| `finance_control_totals.csv` | Signed-off Sales, GP and EBITDA per financial year. Your numbers must tie to these |

The financial year runs 1 March to the end of February. FY26 is March 2025 to February 2026.

## Requirements (acceptance criteria)

- [ ] An ETL step loads every extract into a warehouse (DuckDB or SQL Server). Raw data is landed untouched before any cleaning.
- [ ] Running the load twice must not create duplicates.
- [ ] Cleaned data is modelled as a star schema: fact tables for sales, operating costs and budget, with shared date, branch and item dimensions.
- [ ] Sales, Gross Profit and EBITDA per financial year tie to `finance_control_totals.csv` to the rand. Show the reconciliation.
- [ ] At least 6 automated data checks that fail loudly.
- [ ] Power BI report with these pages: Executive summary, Branch performance, Margin and product mix, Branch P&L.
- [ ] All measures written in DAX, in a dedicated measures table, with a fiscal calendar that starts in March.
- [ ] Like-for-like growth is a measure, not a manual filter.
- [ ] An Excel workbook that reproduces the FY26 branch P&L from the warehouse with a pivot table, as an independent check on the dashboard.
- [ ] A one-page written summary of findings for the Financial Manager, in plain language, with a recommended action per finding.
- [ ] A README someone else could follow to rebuild everything.

## Warning from the person who built the Excel pack

"Do not trust these exports. I know of at least **9 separate problems** across the files. A few will stop your load. Most will load fine and quietly give you the wrong Sales or GP figure, which is worse. If your total does not match finance's control total, the data is not clean yet."

## Stretch goals (only after everything above works)

- Row-level security so a franchisee sees only their own branch.
- A What-if parameter: the effect on group GP of a 1 point price increase.
- Scheduled refresh, or a script that reruns the whole pipeline with one command.
- A weekly flash report page: last week against the same week last year.

## Rules

- Do not open `_answer_key/` until your own numbers are final. It holds the expected figures and the list of data problems.
- Write down every assumption. When a definition is ambiguous (for example, whether refunds reduce sales), decide, document it and move on.
